The short answer

The lovely news is that you won't need to create a formula from scratch. UK labs already have safe, tested sulphate-free formulas, and they'd be happy to put your beautiful 76 label on them.

1
"Private label"

A UK lab's ready-made formula with your own label and branding. It's the quickest and safest way to begin.

2
Start small

Some suppliers start from just 6 or 12 bottles, so you and the team could try them with clients first, then order more once you love them.

3
Stay UK

UK suppliers already look after the UK safety report and notification, which keeps things easy for you. With suppliers from abroad, those steps would be left with you.

4
Keep "76" in the name

Your clients already know and trust the 76 name, so it would feel right at home on both salon shelves.

Your 5 products

What we could ask each supplier for.

Shampoo

No SLS / SLES (the harsh sulphates). Gentle and colour-safe, good for blondes and extensions.

Conditioner

Colour-safe and extension-safe, light and rinses clean.

Hair mask

A rich repair mask, a lovely add-on after colour or blonde appointments.

Growth oil

Usually rosemary + peppermint in a light oil (argan, almond). There's a little note on wording in the UK rules below.

Dry shampoo

Powder (travel-friendly) or aerosol spray (only some labs make it). Dry shampoo usually has no sulphates anyway.

UK rules in plain words

Please don't worry, your supplier usually helps with most of this. It's just nice to know.

Responsible PersonEvery cosmetic sold in Great Britain needs one, with a real UK address (no PO box). Most private label suppliers do this or help with it.
Safety report (CPSR)Each product needs a safety report from a qualified safety assessor.
NotificationEach product goes on the government's online portal (SCPN) before it's sold.
LabelIngredients list, Responsible Person's name and UK address, batch number, how long it lasts once opened, size and any warnings. The supplier normally gives you this text.
Lovely "free-from" wordingIn the UK, product claims need to be backed up, so a gentle suggestion is to use clear words like "sulphate-free", "paraben-free" and "gentle on colour" on labels and posts, once the supplier confirms them. They sound just as lovely and are easy to stand behind.
Growth oil wordingWords like "regrows hair" would need medical proof, so a softer, lovely way to say it is "for a healthy scalp" or "for stronger-looking hair". Clients will still understand exactly what it's for.
